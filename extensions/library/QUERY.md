# Library query revision 2

The public `POST /v1/search` and `GET /v1/documents/{id}/evidence` response shapes remain unchanged. Search uses the existing restricted native reader. For an actual `NativeClient`, every query variant issues one permission-aware `GET /api/documents/?search=...&page_size=24` through `request`; it never follows `next` or invokes `pages()`. The old `search()` reader interface remains for synthetic and legacy test readers.

## Retrieval

At most eight deterministic variants retain precise English words and multiword phrases, fixed domain aliases, and Chinese keyterms with traditional/simplified OpenCC conversions when available. Chinese question filler is removed before keyterm selection; full keyterm chunks from each part of a question precede shorter fragments so a later precise term can reach native search. The variant budget reserves two places for Chinese terms when the question mixes languages. No source text supplies aliases. Queries longer than 512 characters are rejected before any native call.

Each variant fetches one page of at most 24 list rows and contributes at most 12 candidate IDs. Across variants, at most 96 unique IDs enter ranking and at most 32 document detail reads occur. Final ranking uses raw source evidence, full phrases, title agreement, English terms, and distinct keyterm coverage. Repeated windows and low document IDs do not increase score. Every retained match has an original-content excerpt. Every distinct English concept must be supported by its exact term or a fixed static alias across the returned set; an alias entry with no alternatives still requires its original term. A document title may establish a concept only when that same document has an independently matching SOURCE or OCR excerpt. If the source does not itself contain a queried English concept or alias, a matching Chinese keyterm of at least three characters is required in the raw passage; a generic two-character time fragment is insufficient. Thus an unknown precise English term cannot be supplied by unrelated Chinese question fragments, while different articles may each establish one concept. Coverage-aware result selection preserves those articles within the requested limit. At most eight matches return.

Private or deictic credential requests return `no_match` even if an article discusses tokens or passwords. This is a deliberately narrow rule for questions asking for the requester's own secret, not a general credential-topic ban. Search is lexical and bounded: a relevant document beyond the native first page, the per-variant candidate cap, or the 32-read cap can be missed. No semantic embedding, whole-corpus scan, or private-account secret lookup is performed.

## Evidence and citations

Evidence is sliced at exact offsets from the unmodified native `content` string. Legacy appended analysis is excluded. Marked `SOURCE` and `OCR` bodies are separate sections; unmarked legacy content is `source`. The native analysis custom field and marker lines cannot become excerpts. Windows are scored by distinct anchors and include near-end occurrences. Each excerpt is at most 1,200 characters; at most eight excerpts and 9,600 excerpt characters return for a document. The entire evidence response is also at most 12,000 serialized JSON characters, measured with Unicode escapes to cover the actual UTF-8 response. If repeated metadata or a long query would exceed that limit, lower-ranked complete windows are removed; if even one complete hit cannot fit, `hits` is empty and `no_match` is true. Eight hits remain when they fit. `Evidence.as_dict` hashes each exact excerpt.

Direct evidence for one document can show that document's supported portion of a multi-concept question; search checks that the returned collection covers all concepts. Title metadata never becomes an excerpt. A dated article can establish only its historical claim and publication date. Whether a time-limited offer remains valid today is **unverified** without a separate current source.

Author, publication date, fetched date, and completeness come only from library fields or compatible `wx_*` metadata. Native creation timestamps do not become publication dates. Both current and legacy citation URLs pass through the shared `canonical_url` helper after query-side filtering. Citation queries retain known identity keys and remove WeChat sharing/tracking keys, other unknown query keys, and credential-like keys. Unsafe or malformed URLs yield a null citation. This conservative query allowlist may omit an unfamiliar but legitimate identity parameter; source ingestion owns the shared URL helper.

Synthetic tests cover distinct-concept negatives, cross-article coverage, title metadata plus dated Chinese source evidence, retrieval priority, Chinese precise terms behind generic candidates, cross-script budget, no-answer, permission errors, bounded native calls, raw offsets and hashes, sections, near-end excerpts, full JSON and query caps, and citation filtering.
